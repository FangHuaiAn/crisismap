---

There is a lack of systematic and serious planning within maritime Southeast Asian capitals regarding a possible United States–China conflict over Taiwan, despite countries recognising the strategic implications of such a possibility. Most policy discussions in recent years have largely revolved around potential non-combatant evacuation operations (NEOs) of hundreds of thousands of citizens of Southeast Asian countries in Taiwan.
Maritime Southeast Asian states would be unlikely to rely heavily on Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)-related diplomatic mechanisms during a possible US–China conflict over Taiwan, partly due to the grouping’s entrenched crisis collective-action problem. This paper thus proposes a ‘building blocks’ approach at the domestic, bilateral and multilateral levels to enhance Southeast Asia’s – and by implication ASEAN’s – regional crisis-response architecture.
The domestic set of building blocks revolves around individual Southeast Asian states’ crisis wherewithal: their literacy, experience, consensus and capacity. Any regional crisis-response architecture building should begin at home.
The bilateral set of building blocks focuses on whether and how individual Southeast Asian states could leverage their respective bilateral relations with China, the US and Taiwan to begin preliminary conversations around crisis planning before a possible US–China conflict erupts.
The ASEAN set of building blocks requires assessing which ASEAN-related mechanisms could be further enhanced without major overhaul so that the grouping could play a meaningful role during a major security crisis. These blocks also involve considering the possibility of intra-ASEAN minilateral arrangements over NEO contingency planning.

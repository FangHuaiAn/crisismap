# Improving Diffusion of Clinical Care Innovations in Public Health Emergencies: 5 Things Influential Emergency Department Clinicians on Digital Media Can Do

**來源：** RAND
**日期：** 2026-02-26
**URL：** https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4441-3.html

Before Public Health Emergencies
Disclose credentials, affiliations,  
and conflicts of interest, and advocate  
that other influential clinicians  
do the same.
Failing to disclose and misleading the public about credentials and 
conflicts of interest (real or perceived, financial or otherwise) are 
sources of public mistrust in guidance and advice from experts. 
Traditional information sources  (e.g., peer-reviewed journals) have 
established processes for reporting affiliations and credentials and 
disclosing conflicts  of interest, but similar standards have not been 
widely  adopted on digital media platforms.1 By being transparent  
about their credentials, affiliations, and conflicts of  interest, 
influential ED clinicians can promote trust in  the information and 
guidance they share.
Establish collaborative relationships  
with professional societies, journals, 
and other trustworthy sources to amplify 
evidence-based information. 
It is important to create content on evidence-based  
information, including the latest scientific findings and  
clinical expertise on treatment options, benefits, and risks.   
In preparation for a rapidly evolving evidence environment 
during a future PHE, having relationships with reliable,  
vetted sources that are both up-to-date and expert on the topic 
could ensure that content is based on the latest evidence.  
Given the high volumes of often contradictory information  
on digital media, it is important to boost the visibility  
of trustworthy information, including primary sources.
During the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) public health emergency (PHE), emergency department (ED) clinicians on the front  
lines faced supply and staffing shortages, surges in patient volumes, and an evidence cycle that was outpaced by the spread of the 
pandemic. Despite these challenges, many ED clinicians quickly developed and disseminated information on new care innovations, and 
many EDs rapidly adopted and implemented them. The diffusion of these care innovations was uneven, however, and some EDs were 
faster and more effective than others in identifying, trialing, and, when necessary, discontinuing such innovations. Effectively and rapidly 
diffusing knowledge and innovation across diverse practice settings is a key priority for addressing future PHEs. To support ED clinicians 
in future PHEs, this document offers practical strategies that influential ED clinicians on digital media platforms (podcasts, blogs, social 
media, etc.) can take to help other ED clinicians before and during a PHE to identify and spread effective innovations and discontinue 
ineffective or potentially harmful ones. These strategies were developed by RAND in partnership with emergency care experts.
During Public Health Emergencies
Share and amplify content about promising 
new innovations as early as possible,  
but include caveats about uncertainty,  
risk, and local context.
Anecdotal information on what was and was not working 
in clinical care at the earliest stages of the pandemic was 
important in the absence of scientific evidence on new 
innovations.2 Experiences of clinicians from geographies and 
organizations that were especially hard-hit were extremely 
valuable as COVID-19 spread.3 Anecdotal evidence becomes less 
valuable as more-rigorous evidence emerges, so it is critical to 
acknowledge limitations with respect to uncertainty, risk, and 
local context and to provide updates  
as new information becomes available. 
Create multimedia "how-to" content to 
explain and show how care innovations  
can be implemented in practice.
Medical information is traditionally transmitted through writ-
ten content in peer-reviewed journals or textbooks. Multimedia 
how-to content (such as step-by-step instructions, illustrations, 
examples, and demonstrations) can be especially helpful to 
clinicians implementing new care innovations, especially if they 
require multiple cognitive and psychomotor skills.4 Influential 
ED clinicians could consider partnering with journals, research-
ers, or professional societies to help effectively spread pragmatic 
how-to content for care innovations. 
Create content that teaches other clinicians 
how to constructively engage patients in 
making evidence-informed decisions. 
During a PHE, ED clinicians could benefit from specific  
talking points, scripts, and other resources that help  
them enhance trust and support their patients in making 
evidence-informed decisions within a chaotic and  
shifting information environment.

■  How These Strategies Were Created
Over the course of a four-year study, RAND researchers synthesized lessons learned from the dissemination and implementation of ED care 
innovations during the COVID-19 pandemic to develop strategies for more-effective innovation diffusion in future PHEs.5 To develop these 
strategies, the team analyzed qualitative and quantitative data collected through multiple research activities. Some data were drawn from 13 
focus groups (comprising a total of 17 physicians, seven advanced practice providers, 18 registered nurses, and seven respiratory therapists) held 
between November 2022 and June 2023 at five hospital-based EDs purposively sampled from different U.S. regions. Additional data were collected 
through 14 interviews held December 2024 through September 2025 with key disseminators of ED care innovations intentionally selected from 
peer-reviewed literature, preprint platforms, professional society communications, and social media during the course of the COVID-19 pandemic. 
The team extracted findings from the qualitative data on barriers, facilitators, and strategies for the dissemination and implementation of ED 
care innovations. The team also reviewed quantitative findings from archived discourse on ED care innovations from April 2022 through May 
2024 and conducted a nationwide survey from February through September 2024 of 808 physicians and 822 nurses who were involved in clinical 
care during the pandemic. During a two-day, in-person meeting in October 2025, both inductive and deductive approaches (e.g., pile-sorting and 
role-playing) were used to develop strategies for different stakeholder groups to improve the diffusion of ED care innovations, particularly during 
PHEs. An expert panel of ED professionals from a variety of clinical backgrounds provided feedback from November 2025 through February 2026 
to inform the final list of strategies. 
This document presents strategies specifically aimed at influential ED clinicians on digital media platforms. Although there are many ways that such 
clinicians can help improve management and response in future PHEs, the strategies presented here focus on improving the diffusion of innovations 
for delivering clinical care in EDs; other aspects of PHE response (e.g., communications with the public aside from ED encounters) were considered 
out of scope.

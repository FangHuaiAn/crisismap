# Improving Diffusion of Clinical Care Innovations in Public Health Emergencies: 4 Things State and Local Public Health Officials Can Do

**來源：** RAND
**日期：** 2026-02-26
**URL：** https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4441-6.html

Before Public Health Emergencies
Establish or maintain lines of 
communication and regular touchpoints 
with other state and local health 
departments and local or regional  
health care systems.
Public health departments serve an important role not only 
in aggregating population health information at the national 
level but also in translating and adapting national guidance to 
regional and local contexts. It is easier to leverage established 
relationships and communication infrastructure than to 
form new ones in a PHE. Creating quarterly or semiannual 
touchpoints with other state and local health departments and 
local or regional health care systems strengthens communication 
channels and provides opportunities to discuss and address  
local public health conditions and recommended care 
approaches. Regular touchpoints also help establish an 
infrastructure and relationships that can be quickly activated  
or ramped up in a PHE, enabling agencies to align guidance 
and recommendations, coordinate limited resources, and share 
information on promising clinical care innovations.
Cultivate relationships with local ED 
clinicians to enhance trust in public 
institutions and public health guidance.
Public trust in information is essential for ensuring widespread 
adherence to public health recommendations during a PHE,1 
and public health officials often must rely on clinicians to 
communicate information to patients. Establishing connections 
between public health agencies and local ED clinicians  
(e.g., through regular presentations to ED clinicians on local 
public health conditions and recommended care approaches) can 
foster this trust and can facilitate the timely sharing  
of information during a PHE.2
During the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) public health emergency (PHE), emergency department (ED) clinicians on the front lines 
faced supply and staffing shortages, surges in patient volumes, and an evidence cycle that was outpaced by the spread of the pandemic. 
Despite these challenges, many ED clinicians quickly developed and disseminated information on new care innovations, and many  
EDs rapidly adopted and implemented them. The diffusion of these care innovations was uneven, however, and some EDs were faster 
and more effective than others in identifying, trialing, and, when necessary, discontinuing such innovations. Effectively and rapidly 
diffusing knowledge and innovation across diverse practice settings is a key priority for addressing future PHEs. To support ED clinicians 
in future PHEs, this document offers practical strategies that state and local public health officials can take before and during a PHE to 
identify and spread effective innovations and discontinue ineffective or potentially harmful ones. These strategies were developed by 
RAND in partnership with emergency care experts.
During Public Health Emergencies
Facilitate a local data- and information-
sharing initiative to promote real-time 
learning across health systems.
Public health agencies are often the only entities positioned to 
connect fragmented local health care systems that are typically 
not equipped, prepared, or inclined to share information with 
each other, especially during a PHE. Leaders of health care 
systems are often reluctant to publicly report data as a result 
of resource constraints, privacy or legal concerns, and possible 
reputational consequences.3 Health systems also differ in their 
approaches to managing data, so some degree of standardization 
is necessary to enable effective sharing across organizations. 
Aggregating data on care innovations from local health 
care systems into a registry or data-sharing platform could 
improve diffusion and encourage participation while reducing 
the perceived risks associated with public data reporting by 
individual health systems. 
Provide ED leaders and clinicians with 
locally tailored guidance and resources 
to engage constructively with patients 
in evidence-informed decisionmaking 
at the bedside.
Clinicians are patients’ most-trusted information source,4 
but they might not be aware of the types of messages on 
care innovations and treatments prevalent among their 
patient populations. The relationships of local public health 
departments with community stakeholders and trusted 
messengers could yield insight into specific conceptions and 
misconceptions on treatments as they change during the  
chaotic and shifting information environment of a PHE.  
By providing frontline clinicians with locally tailored guidance 
and resources, public health agencies could help ED clinicians 
enhance both trust and evidence-informed decisionmaking  
with patients during PHEs.

■  How These Strategies Were Created
Over the course of a four-year study, RAND researchers synthesized lessons learned from the dissemination and implementation of ED care 
innovations during the COVID-19 pandemic to develop strategies for more-effective innovation diffusion in future PHEs.5 To develop these 
strategies, the team analyzed qualitative and quantitative data collected through multiple research activities. Some data were drawn from 13 
focus groups (comprising a total of 17 physicians, seven advanced practice providers, 18 registered nurses, and seven respiratory therapists) held 
between November 2022 and June 2023 at five hospital-based EDs purposively sampled from different U.S. regions. Additional data were collected 
through 14 interviews held December 2024 through September 2025 with key disseminators of ED care innovations intentionally selected from 
peer-reviewed literature, preprint platforms, professional society communications, and social media during the course of the COVID-19 pandemic. 
The team extracted findings from the qualitative data on barriers, facilitators, and strategies for the dissemination and implementation of ED 
care innovations. The team also reviewed quantitative findings from archived discourse on ED care innovations from April 2022 through May 
2024 and conducted a nationwide survey from February through September 2024 of 808 physicians and 822 nurses who were involved in clinical 
care during the pandemic. During a two-day, in-person meeting in October 2025, both inductive and deductive approaches (e.g., pile-sorting and 
role-playing) were used to develop strategies for different stakeholder groups to improve the diffusion of ED care innovations, particularly during 
PHEs. An expert panel of ED professionals from a variety of clinical backgrounds provided feedback from November 2025 through February 2026 
to inform the final list of strategies. 
This document presents strategies specifically aimed at state and local public health officials. Although there are many ways that such officials can 
help improve management and response in future PHEs, the strategies presented here focus on improving the diffusion of innovations for delivering 
clinical care in EDs; other aspects of PHE response (e.g., communications with the public aside from ED encounters) were considered out of scope.

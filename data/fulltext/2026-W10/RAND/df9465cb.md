# Improving Diffusion of Clinical Care Innovations in Public Health Emergencies: 5 Things Emergency Department and Health System Leaders Can Do

**來源：** RAND
**日期：** 2026-02-26
**URL：** https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4441-1.html

Before Public Health Emergencies
Create or participate in communication 
networks locally, regionally, and 
nationally. 
It is critical to develop trusting relationships and lines of 
communication within a health system and with other health 
system stakeholders in the region before a PHE occurs. Both 
inter- and intra-system communication channels are important 
for sharing information about best practices and balancing 
resources and capacity. These networks could meet quarterly 
or semiannually, scaling back or ramping up engagement as 
needed. National forums are particularly important channels for 
exchanging care innovations and strategies for managing PHEs 
when they occur.1 State and local forums organized through 
health systems, public health agencies, and other partners can 
be important channels for sharing information about regional 
conditions and care innovations. 
Identify a team and process to  
create living, organization-specific 
guidance based on the best  
available evidence for clinicians. 
During a PHE, frontline clinicians need support and  
guidance on what is known about best practices even when 
evidence and information are limited. Conditions and 
knowledge change rapidly, so ensuring that information can 
be updated quickly and shared widely across the organization 
is critical. Within the ED, having a dedicated team and  
process in place to vet new treatment evidence and guidance 
can speed adoption and consensus on care innovations.  
Using online tools that streamline editing and allow users 
to easily see when guidance was last updated and view prior 
versions can foster transparency and trust during rapidly  
changing conditions.
During the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) public health emergency (PHE), emergency department (ED) clinicians on the front 
lines faced supply and staffing shortages, surges in patient volumes, and an evidence cycle that was outpaced by the spread of the 
pandemic. Despite these challenges, many ED clinicians quickly developed and disseminated information on new care innovations, and 
many EDs rapidly adopted and implemented them. The diffusion of these care innovations was uneven, however, and some EDs were 
faster and more effective than others in identifying, trialing, and, when necessary, discontinuing such innovations. Effectively and  
rapidly diffusing knowledge and innovation across diverse practice settings is a key priority for addressing future PHEs. To support  
ED clinicians in future PHEs, this document offers practical strategies that ED and health system leaders can take before and during  
a PHE to identify and spread effective innovations and discontinue ineffective or potentially harmful ones. These strategies were 
developed by RAND in partnership with emergency care experts.
During Public Health Emergencies
Support the creation of materials  
to help clinicians implement  
care innovations. 
Such tools as templates, order sets, decision trees, protocols, 
and visuals can lower barriers to adoption of care innovations 
and help distill complex information into simple steps. ED and 
health system leaders can provide dedicated time or resources 
for staff to develop and spread these materials.
Create venues and opportunities to  
facilitate ongoing two-way communi-
cation between leadership and staff  
on management of the PHE. 
Communicating directly with staff is important for building 
trust and offers a way to learn what is (or is not) working for 
staff and patients. Venues could be briefings, town halls, 
webinars, listening sessions, discussion boards, clinical rounds, 
and handoffs. Communication should occur regularly according 
to the pace of new information and bandwidth of staff. Whether 
these communications are conducted in person or virtually, 
opportunities should be afforded not only for leaders to provide 
information to staff but also for staff to ask questions and 
provide feedback—both with leaders and with each other. 
Create dashboards or other ways  
to monitor and update staff on 
operating conditions. 
Real-time information on an organization’s conditions (such as 
patient volumes, staffing, resources, and care quality) can help 
staff understand and anticipate care innovations that might 
be needed or discontinued (e.g., reuse of personal protective 
equipment) and monitor what is (or is not) working.

■  How These Strategies Were Created
Over the course of a four-year study, RAND researchers synthesized lessons learned from the dissemination and implementation of ED care 
innovations during the COVID-19 pandemic to develop strategies for more-effective innovation diffusion in future PHEs.2 To develop these 
strategies, the team analyzed qualitative and quantitative data collected through multiple research activities. Some data were drawn from 13 
focus groups (comprising a total of 17 physicians, seven advanced practice providers, 18 registered nurses, and seven respiratory therapists) held 
between November 2022 and June 2023 at five hospital-based EDs purposively sampled from different U.S. regions. Additional data were collected 
through 14 interviews held December 2024 through September 2025 with key disseminators of ED care innovations intentionally selected from 
peer-reviewed literature, preprint platforms, professional society communications, and social media during the course of the COVID-19 pandemic. 
The team extracted findings from the qualitative data on barriers, facilitators, and strategies for the dissemination and implementation of ED 
care innovations. The team also reviewed quantitative findings from archived discourse on ED care innovations from April 2022 through May 
2024 and conducted a nationwide survey from February through September 2024 of 808 physicians and 822 nurses who were involved in clinical 
care during the pandemic. During a two-day, in-person meeting in October 2025, both inductive and deductive approaches (e.g., pile-sorting and 
role-playing) were used to develop strategies for different stakeholder groups to improve the diffusion of ED care innovations, particularly during 
PHEs. An expert panel of ED professionals from a variety of clinical backgrounds provided feedback from November 2025 through February 2026 
to inform the final list of strategies. 
This document presents strategies specifically aimed at ED and health system leaders. Although there are many ways that such leaders can help 
improve management and response in future PHEs, the strategies presented here focus on improving the diffusion of innovations for delivering 
clinical care in EDs; other aspects of PHE response (e.g., communications with the public aside from ED encounters) were considered out of scope.

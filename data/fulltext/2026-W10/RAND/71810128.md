# Improving Diffusion of Clinical Care Innovations in Public Health Emergencies: 6 Things Frontline Emergency Department Clinicians Can Do

**來源：** RAND
**日期：** 2026-02-26
**URL：** https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4441-2.html

---

Before Public Health Emergencies
Participate in or create informal, 
interprofessional information-sharing 
networks. 
Care innovations spread via word of mouth. Clinical 
colleagues are among the most-trusted sources of information 
on care innovations, but disciplines and service lines 
within the same organization might be siloed from one 
another. Organizing or participating in (virtual or in-person) 
interprofessional information-sharing networks can help 
spread awareness of care innovations that are promising or 
working well and those that should be discontinued.
Get involved in professional 
governance, such as by supporting 
or advocating within local or national 
professional societies. 
A key role of professional societies is to provide consistent, 
evidence-based guidance for frontline clinicians by leveraging 
the collective expertise of their members and other experts. 
These groups rely on professional participation and member 
support to develop and share timely care guidance, especially 
during PHEs.
Identify and follow trustworthy, 
influential ED clinicians and other 
digital media sources. 
Credible experts and organizations share information on care 
innovations through digital media platforms, such as podcasts, 
blogs, and social media (e.g., via the Free Open Access Medical 
Education [FOAMed] movement).1 Consider finding and 
vetting such sources before PHEs. 
During the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) public health emergency (PHE), emergency department (ED) clinicians on the front 
lines faced supply and staffing shortages, surges in patient volumes, and an evidence cycle that was outpaced by the spread of the 
pandemic. Despite these challenges, many ED clinicians quickly developed and disseminated information on new care innovations,  
and many EDs rapidly adopted and implemented them. The diffusion of these care innovations was uneven, however, and some  
EDs were faster and more effective than others in identifying, trialing, and, when necessary, discontinuing such innovations. Effectively 
and rapidly diffusing knowledge and innovation across diverse practice settings is a key priority for addressing future PHEs. To  
support ED clinicians in future PHEs, this document offers practical strategies that frontline ED clinicians can take before and during  
a PHE to identify and spread effective innovations and discontinue ineffective or potentially harmful ones. These strategies were 
developed by RAND in partnership with emergency care experts.
During Public Health Emergencies
Engage in or create new opportunities 
for sharing information with peers and 
institutional leadership. 
Frontline clinicians have critical information for ED 
and health system leaders on what is (or is not) working, 
particularly during an evolving PHE. Participation in such 
opportunities as virtual learning collaboratives and webinars 
will help in keeping up with the best available information 
and spreading it to peers and institutional leaders. Colleagues 
at the nexus of multiple clinical communities (students and 
residents working across units, clinicians with multiple jobs, 
traveling clinicians, and double-boarded clinicians) are critical 
connectors of different care environments, bringing new 
information and ideas from other environments.
Take steps to measure, report, and 
act on lessons from implementing 
emerging care innovations. 
Assessing outcomes can help organizations make better 
decisions about which care innovations to continue, modify,  
or discontinue.
Engage constructively with patients 
at the bedside to support evidence-
informed decisionmaking. 
Clinicians are patients’ most-trusted information sources but 
might not have the knowledge or skills to effectively engage 
patients in making evidence-informed decisions in a chaotic 
and shifting information environment.2 Discussions might 
be particularly challenging for clinicians and patients if care 
options are the subject of politicized debates in popular or 
digital media outlets. Clinicians should seek out best practices 
and resources they can use to enhance trust and guide 
decisionmaking with patients during ED encounters.

■  How These Strategies Were Created
Over the course of a four-year study, RAND researchers synthesized lessons learned from the dissemination and implementation of ED care 
innovations during the COVID-19 pandemic to develop strategies for more-effective innovation diffusion in future PHEs.3 To develop these 
strategies, the team analyzed qualitative and quantitative data collected through multiple research activities. Some data were drawn from 13 
focus groups (comprising a total of 17 physicians, seven advanced practice providers, 18 registered nurses, and seven respiratory therapists) held 
between November 2022 and June 2023 at five hospital-based EDs purposively sampled from different U.S. regions. Additional data were collected 
through 14 interviews held December 2024 through September 2025 with key disseminators of ED care innovations intentionally selected from 
peer-reviewed literature, preprint platforms, professional society communications, and social media during the course of the COVID-19 pandemic. 
The team extracted findings from the qualitative data on barriers, facilitators, and strategies for the dissemination and implementation of ED 
care innovations. The team also reviewed quantitative findings from archived discourse on ED care innovations from April 2022 through May 
2024 and conducted a nationwide survey from February through September 2024 of 808 physicians and 822 nurses who were involved in clinical 
care during the pandemic. During a two-day, in-person meeting in October 2025, both inductive and deductive approaches (e.g., pile-sorting and 
role-playing) were used to develop strategies for different stakeholder groups to improve the diffusion of ED care innovations, particularly during 
PHEs. An expert panel of ED professionals from a variety of clinical backgrounds provided feedback from November 2025 through February 2026 
to inform the final list of strategies. 
This document presents strategies specifically aimed at frontline ED clinicians. Although there are many ways that such clinicians can help improve 
management and response in future PHEs, the strategies presented here focus on improving the diffusion of innovations for delivering clinical care 
in EDs; other aspects of PHE response (e.g., communications with the public aside from ED encounters) were considered out of scope.
